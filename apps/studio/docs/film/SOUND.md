# SOUND — ambience, music, and the decade split

Sound sells the period more cheaply than picture does. A 90s ring tone and a
room-tone bed make a phone call feel like 1995 before anyone looks at the
phone.

## Room tone you already recorded (use before any library)

| file | what | use under |
|---|---|---|
| STE-000 | church ambience | S1, S4 |
| STE-109 | church ambience, part 2 | S1, S4 |
| STE-057, STE-058 | theatre ambience | S5, P1, P2 |

For locations with no recorded tone (apartment, rooftop, street), pull
1–2 s of the quietest stretch between lines from that scene's own recorder
files and loop it with crossfades. Never leave digital silence under
dialogue; the cut will be audible.

## Decade map

- **80s, childhood (S1–S4).** Stone reverb, distant birds, a church bell,
  muffled traffic with no modern engine or EV whine, footsteps and door
  echoes slightly exaggerated (the spec asks for this).
- **90s, teen and after (S5 onward).** City bed, a distant siren, older car
  engines, a 90s phone ring for N8 (a mechanical-bell or early electronic
  trill, not a smartphone tone), rooftop wind and far-off party music.

Remove or cover anything anachronistic that sound can reveal: phone
notification chirps, modern car alerts, and electric scooters.

## Music

- **The guitar motif is on screen:** there is an acoustic guitar in Max's
  apartment (C0000–C0007). The motif can start there, played in the room
  (diegetic), and come back as score at the audition's success and the
  play's curtain call.
- **80s palette under childhood:** sparse analog-synth pad, music box or
  electric piano, very little.
- **90s palette:** acoustic guitar, light drums, electric piano.
- **Rights:** use original or properly licensed music only. Anything
  generated or taken from a library needs its licence recorded next to the
  file.

## Cue rules for proposals

- Every cue is anchored to an event id (see AI_GUIDE §4), never to absolute
  time.
- Music and ambience duck under dialogue. Sound effects do not.
- Transitions that jolt get a sound bridge: bring the next scene's ambience
  in 0.5–1 s before the picture cut (a J-cut), or let the current one tail
  out after it (an L-cut).
- Give each cue a one-line reason. "Bell on the cut to S1: establishes the
  church before we see it" can be checked; "adds atmosphere" cannot.

## Narration

Narration is optional; the director hasn't decided. If used, draw from the
end-monologue drafts in
`supporting-documents/scene-plans/scripts/unsorted/end-monologue-pitch.md`
(the writer found v1 r0 the most emotional). Lines should comment, not
explain.

A candidate structure: the monologue's first line over the opening
applause, and its last lines over the curtain call, so it bookends the film.
