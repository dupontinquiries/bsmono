ANC research findings & V1 plan

Visual recap of the noise-cancellation research, now with cross-reference against a real street recording.

The V1 status CRITICAL

Cross-referencing the canceller against a 40-second real-world engine recording revealed that most of the experimental wins on synthetic engine signals were artifacts of the textbook synth being too easy. Real engines have 8+ substantial harmonics with h2 often louder than the fundamental; the canceller tracks 3 harmonics with fixed [1.0, 0.5, 0.25] amplitudes.

What this means for V1:

  • Ship the masker — music masker is genuinely useful, validated, 39.8° annoyance tolerance, no phase coherence required.
  • Keep the canceller in research mode — it works on textbook synth but not on real engines yet.
  • The architectural path is clear — 8-harmonic extension with measured amplitudes — but the implementation has a bug that's preventing it from approaching the empirically-measured 30 dB headroom.

Real engines have h2 louder than the fundamental (1.30 vs 1.00) — typical of 4-stroke even-cylinder vehicles where even harmonics are phase-aligned by firing order. h3 is suppressed (the matching odd-harmonic dip), then h4 through h7 are all substantial (0.5-0.9 of the fundamental).

The canceller's 3-harmonic anti-noise with fixed [1.0, 0.5, 0.25] amplitudes covers maybe 30% of the actual energy. The other 70% is in h4-h7 where we don't even try.

4. Findings that survived the real-recording cross-check

Some findings hold up regardless of synth realism:

  • Latency vs jitter — Constant latency is harmless; jitter destroys phase coherence. Still true on real signals.
  • Per-harmonic clipping — Independent soft-clip on each harmonic avoids intermodulation distortion. Still true.
  • Geometry-aware Doppler gate — Real recording's fundamental track jumps around (70-400 Hz with no clean Doppler curve) — confirms the relaxed gate is needed even more than I thought.
  • Reverb finding — The recording's estimated RT60 is in the 300-2000 ms range depending on which segment you measure. Our reverb experiments underestimated rather than overestimated.

5. Findings that were artifacts of textbook synth

  • +9.78 dB passby suppression — only on textbook signal with 3 harmonics. On realistic 8-harmonic signal, current canceller is -0.32 dB.
  • +2.38 dB saturation matching — the per-harmonic-clipping fix is real but its measured benefit was inflated by textbook simplicity.
  • Per-harmonic phase lock comparisons — the 2.5 dB advantage of fixed offsets at "ideal conditions" was for a 3-harmonic source. On 8-harmonic real signals, the comparison is different (both modes near zero).
The pattern is now 5× confirmed: theoretical wins on synthetic signals are unreliable; cross-reference against real recordings is necessary before drawing conclusions.

6. The v1 architecture target (post-v1 work)

For the canceller to actually deliver on real engines, the architecture needs:

  1. Track N harmonics, where N is 6-10 with adaptive detection of "live" harmonics (skip silent ones to save compute).
  2. Measure amplitudes per window via Goertzel — no fixed [1.0, 0.5, 0.25] profile. Real engine amplitudes vary by vehicle type, RPM, distance.
  3. Sub-degree phase accuracy — a 16-trial fundamental search gives ±π/16 (0.2 rad) accuracy, which becomes π/2 (random) at h8. Goertzel-derived phase or finer-grained search needed.
  4. Per-harmonic amplitude scaling in synthesis — emit anti-noise with measured amplitude profile, not textbook ratios.

The implementation is in passby_canceller.rs (set_n_harmonics, refresh_extended_harmonics, etc.) but currently delivers only -0.15 dB vs the -0.83 dB legacy. The ratio suggests the architecture is correct but the phase/amplitude application chain has a bug.

7. The recurring pattern, now 5× confirmed

  1. Octave shifting — needs gain ≥ 2.0, doubles total power.
  2. Sum-then-clip saturation — algorithm choice matters; clip-each-harmonic is the right form.
  3. Per-harmonic phase lock — conditional, not absolute. Wins under jitter or reverb, loses on clean anechoic.
  4. Sliding-shelf saturation — uniform per-harmonic drive is best; ramping up doesn't help.
  5. 3-harmonic canceller on textbook synth — the +9.78 dB win evaporated when tested on real signal.
Each theoretical bet failed in its specific way. The research process is "make the simulated environment harder until the theoretical wins fall over, then figure out why." The real recording was the final hardness check, and most of the wins fell over.
