# v0.24.101 — physics-driven water wave normals

Replaces the flat, static water surface with an animated wave **normal** driven
by the engine's own wind and temperature fields, then runs the Fresnel + sky
reflection + sun specular you already had against that perturbed normal. The
surface now ripples, the sun glint travels with the waves, and the reflection
breaks up — entirely inside the existing opaque chunk pass (no new render pass,
no Vulkan/pipeline change). Independent of the `UNENDLICH_POSTFX` work.

## What changed
- New `shaders/water_waves.glsl`: an analytic wave field — a 4-octave sum of
  directional Gerstner-lite waves (squared sine for sharper crests), with the
  surface normal taken from the **analytic gradient** computed in the same pass.
- `shaders/chunk.frag`: includes it; the water branch perturbs the flat top
  normal with `water_wave_normal(...)` and feeds that into Fresnel, the
  sky-reflection mix, and the specular highlight. The old scalar brightness
  `wave` is gone (the ripple now comes from the normal).
- `toolkit/harness/RULESET.md` §water: freezes the wave contract.

## Physics, not a generic FBM
Per your steer, the waves are a function of the live fields:
- **wind direction** → wave propagation direction (octaves fanned around it),
- **wind magnitude** → amplitude + speed (calm glassy ↔ windy chop),
- **temperature** → damping toward glassy as water nears freezing, so the
  surface calms just before the freeze system turns it to ice.

Documented future inputs to the same `WaveParams`: humidity / weather (rain
choppiness + ripple rings), season, and an entity-ripple displacement term for
objects pushing the surface around.

## Designed for the eventual goals
- **Entity collision / buoyancy.** The field is a pure function of
  `(XZ, time, wind, temperature)` with no textures and no GPU-only state, so a
  CPU port is a direct transcription. RULESET §water freezes the constants and
  conventions; the planned `water_waves.rs` + `water_waves_parity` test become
  the single source of truth a buoyancy/splash system queries. (Next turn.)
- **SSR churn.** The perturbed wave normal is the normal SSR must reflect off,
  so when reflections land they ripple instead of staying mirror-sharp — which
  is exactly the "churn" you asked for. The contract requires SSR to use this
  normal for water, not the flat face normal.

## Performance
- Pure ALU, no texture noise → no dependent texture fetches (tile-GPU friendly).
- Per-octave wavenumber/speed are compile-time constants (the wavelength
  sequence is constant), so only the phase varies per pixel; the 4 `sqrt`s fold
  away. Cost is ~4×(sin+cos) per water fragment.
- Normal from the analytic gradient = ONE field evaluation, vs the usual 3-tap
  finite difference (~3× cheaper).
- Grazing-angle damping reduces specular aliasing as a side benefit.
- Only runs on water top faces (`normal.y > 0.5`), inside the existing
  `is_water` branch.

## Acceptance / A/B
This is a shader-only change (hot-reload). Water should now visibly ripple with
moving highlights and a broken-up sky reflection; everything else unchanged. To
A/B, flip `WATER_WAVE_NORMALS` in `water_waves.glsl` to `false` and hot-reload —
that restores the flat-normal surface. Tuning knobs are the `WW_*` constants
(amplitude, wavelength, octaves, fan). Watch for: over-bright shimmer at the
horizon (lower the grazing floor), or waves too strong/weak (`WW_BASE_AMP`).
