If you wanted to treat a typical "Minecraft 100 Days" playthrough as a giant integration test for the entire game engine, you can think of it as a coverage benchmark rather than a gameplay video.

The goal is not "did the player survive 100 days?" but:

How much of the engine, simulation, content pipeline, rendering system, AI system, progression system, networking model, and world-generation stack was exercised during the run?

A Claude-Code style analyzer could assign scores across hundreds of categories and produce a coverage report similar to software test coverage.

For an ECS or dependency-wiring focused engine, the interesting metric is often not "what gameplay feature happened?" but:

Which components, systems, resources, events, jobs, pipelines, and graph edges became active?

A Minecraft-style sandbox is actually a huge wiring graph.
