# The Gathering — lore of Clawbound

## The world without hands

There were never any humans. No hand raised a wall, no fire was kindled but
lightning's, and no word was written until the clans learned to tell their
stories in scent-marks, songs and the shapes of claw-cuts in bark. The great
cats inherited the world and kept it — through the frozen long-ago, when ice
lay over half of it and cave lions ruled the steppes; through the elder dawn,
when the prey was the size of hills and the sabre-fangs hunted it; through
the green ages of lion prides, tiger reed-forests and leopard rivers; through
the long winters of the northern taiga and the high crags; across the
endless dunes and into the far-east thunder mountains, where even a storm
has a son.

The clans are many and old. Each keeps its own lands, songs and laws. Each
remembers its **ancestors** — the founders and the great ones — and the
ancestors do not entirely leave. A clan that needs them can call them home.

## The Gathering

Some things cannot be divided and must not be warred over: who keeps the
winter grounds when the herds move, which clan speaks for the rivers in a
drought year, whether an old law still binds. For these, the clans do not go
to war. They hold **the Gathering**.

- All the living clans split into two halves **by lot** — never by kind, so
  that no old feud decides the sides. The halves are named for the two
  halves of the year: **Frostfang**, the cold half, and **Emberclaw**, the
  warm half.
- They meet on **the old grounds** — places no clan owns — and call their
  ancestors home across the ages to stand with them. So a cave lion of the
  frozen long-ago may fight beside a tiger of the green ages, and a
  sabre-tooth against its own descendants.
- Each half is led by its **Elder**, a living clan-mother or clan-father,
  shielded by the half's standing stones (the towers). When the stones fall
  the Elder stands unshielded; when an Elder is brought down, the Gathering
  is decided, and the clans abide by it.
- The **young cats** of each half march the old paths between the stones
  (the minions): the Gathering is also how the young learn the law.
- The old grounds have their own **great beasts** — boars and drakes older
  than the clans — and the land itself sometimes turns against everyone
  (the disasters). Neither takes a side.

What the game shows follows from this: sides drawn at random each match; any
ancestor on either side; teams named for seasons, not species; an Elder to
bring down; stones to break first.

## The old grounds

- **The Green Hollow** (the jungle map): a valley of three old paths through
  deep jungle, rivers and ruins of scent-stones older than any clan. Five a
  side: the Gathering's oldest ground, for small disputes.
- **Twinfang Pass**: a canyon between two peaks shaped like a pair of
  sabre-fangs — named, the clans say, for Twofang, whose clan hunted there in
  the elder dawn. Fifteen a side: for matters that concern every clan at
  once. The high path climbs a shelf around the western fang; the caves along
  the side paths are the dens of the vole-beasts that keep the camps.
- **The Thornmaze**: the works of the **Geartooth clan**, the engineer-cats
  of the old hills, who learned to cut wood and stone with their teeth and
  set the rivers to turn it. They built the labyrinth as a machine for
  moving the mountain: gear trains across the wall tops, stone gates that
  rise and sink in their slots, Archimedes screws that lift quarried stone
  up from the cisterns, and tracks the stones roll down through the rooms.
  The Geartooth are long gone; the machine is not. The thorns grew over it
  after them, and wake where it is working. Five a side.

## The ancestors

Genders: only Stillsmoke's was stated by the game ("the ninth son of the
storm-dragon"). The rest were decided against the game's own data and are
checked by tests in `src/lore.rs`: every maned pelt is a male (Goldenmane;
Oldroar's "ragged mane"), every pronoun in the game's text agrees, and no
voice note contradicts its ancestor. Seven are female, six male.

**Goldenmane.** First of the lion-clan to lead a pride that was not his by
birth. He taught that a cat alone is a whisker and a pride is a storm, and
the plains have been loud with prides ever since.

**Duskstripe.** A tigress who raised four litters alone and taught every one
of them to strike once, from where no one looked. The tiger-clan still hunts
at dusk because she did.

**Rainspots.** A leopard who fished the great rivers and mended the hurt she
found on their banks. She never fought the current; she went around, and the
leopard-clan learned to win that way.

**Nightpelt.** Mother of the shadow-walkers, black as the woods between
moons. She never chased; she learned where prey would choose to run, and was
already there.

**Windclaw.** A young caracal who leapt at birds in the air and caught them,
and so decided the sky could be hunted. The wind-eared clan counts its years
by his hundred leaps.

**Faryowl.** The first of the far-yowlers, whose call crossed the grasslands
before she did. Her clan settles quarrels by song, and she has never lost
one.

**Oldroar.** Lord of the cave-lions when the ice lay over half the world. The
ice took his clan, but not him: the old world, he says, is only sleeping in
him.

**Twofang.** A sabre-tooth of the elder dawn, when the prey was the size of
hills. His claws began the hunt and his fangs ended it. The pass where the
Gathering meets bears his fangs' name.

**Suneye.** High priestess of the sun-cats, who read the dunes by noon light
and kept no secrets from it. The disc above her brow is the sun's promise:
nothing hides.

**Snowhush.** A lynx of the deep snows who never fought where her enemy
chose. She picked the ground, let the snow fall, and waited; her clan keeps
the silence still.

**Riptail.** A jaguar of the flooded dark, who hunted under the water as
easily as over it. The river does not chase, she says; it gathers, and so
does she.

**Stillsmoke.** Ninth son of the storm-dragon: antlered, scaled,
sabre-fanged, and happiest sitting still in incense smoke. He sits so long
that the storm comes to ask his leave — and when he stands, his father's
thunder stands with him. (The dragon's nine sons are an old story of the far
east; one of them, lion-like, loves to sit among incense smoke.)

**Stonewhisper.** The grey ghost of the crags, seldom seen and never late.
She crossed the high passes in every weather; the mountain taught her not to
hurry.

**Cometail.** A caracal who leapt so high after the birds that he caught the
tail of a falling comet, and came down with it onto the glacier. Where he
landed, the Starwell opened -- the spirit-sky of Starwell Glacier, where the
Moonwyrm sleeps in the healing water -- and he has been a little made of
starlight ever since. He never once asked where he might land.

**Sproutkit.** The youngest of the ancestors, and the only one who never
grew up. Every litter born in every clan is a little of him, so there is
always more of him than anyone counted. He turns every fight into a game,
and every game into a dance -- and what dances cannot fight. Where he naps,
the first green of the year comes up through the snow.

**Hollowstep.** Nobody on the moors ever saw Hollowstep arrive. His tread
was heard on one side of the heath and he was found on the other, and the
clans learned to count their own shadows before they slept. He never ran
after anything in his life; he never had to. They say another of him is
still out on the moor tonight, walking, and that you will hear it long
before you see it.

**Brasshowl.** The smallest of the ancestors, with the biggest voice. He
walked ahead of the caravans on the salt roads at night with a brass
lantern, and when something moved at the edge of its light he raised the
lamp and bellowed, and whatever it was did not come in. It is said that
Hollowstep has never once stood in Brasshowl's light, and that the two of
them know exactly why.

**Oremantle.** The first of the **Sentinels**, the great cats the Geartooth
engineers built of stone and ore to keep their works when they were gone.
They built her in the likeness of Stonewhisper's grandmother, of the old
snow leopards that were half as big again as any cat since, who lay so long
on the roots of the crags that the engineers could quarry her shape from the
mountain itself; and they gave her the grandmother's voice. Her coat is
plates on the machine's axles, and turned, it shows what the mountain is
made of: granite in the thaw, rime in the long dark, lodestone where the
iron veins run, and once, when the clans needed it, the molten heart
itself. She never went to a fight in her life. She became what the fight
was standing on. The Geartooth machines know her as their own.

## The music of the clans

For scoring and sound design: each clan's culture has a sound, and the two
halves of the Gathering can be heard in them — Frostfang in the cold
cultures, Emberclaw in the warm, though any ancestor may stand on either
side.

| Ancestor | Instruments | Nature |
|---|---|---|
| Goldenmane | kora, balafon, talking drum, djembe | grass wind, far thunder |
| Duskstripe | bansuri flute, tabla, low drones | frogs, rain on broad leaves |
| Rainspots | mbira, kalimba, water drums, shakers | running water, birdsong |
| Nightpelt | soft gongs, slit drums, bamboo flute | night insects, a far owl |
| Windclaw | ney, bendir frame drum, rattles | hot wind, cicadas |
| Faryowl | layered voices, musical bow, udu | tall grass, weaver birds |
| Oldroar | bone flute, struck stones, deep skin drums, bullroarer, long horns | wind over ice |
| Twofang | clay ocarinas, log drums, rattles | bubbling tar, great hooves |
| Suneye | sistrum, ney, darbuka, oud | desert wind, sand hiss |
| Snowhush | hardanger fiddle, nyckelharpa, kulning, goat horn, frame drum | wind in spruce, creaking ice |
| Riptail | rainstick, panpipes, water percussion, slit drum | rain, floodwater |
| Stillsmoke | guqin, dizi, temple bells, great barrel drums | mountain rain, far thunder |
| Stonewhisper | long mountain horns, singing bowls, damaru, low chant | high wind, falling scree |
| Cometail | glass harmonica, crystal bowls, frame drum, overtone singing | the hiss of a meteor, ice cracking in the cold |
| Sproutkit | toy piano, music box, ukulele, kazoo, handclaps, a disco beat | birdsong, a spring brook |
| Hollowstep | bowed saw, low cello drones, a slow bass drum like a heartbeat, held breath | wind over heather, a curlew far off |
| Brasshowl | frame drum, oud, a brass bell, clapping hands, a caravan's camel bells | desert wind, a crackling lamp |
| Oremantle | the deepest mountain horns, stone chimes, a slow frame drum like footfalls, overtone throat-singing | wind in a cave mouth, rock settling |

**Frostfang** colours: long horns, hardanger fiddle and nyckelharpa, kulning,
bone flute, singing bowls, wind over ice. **Emberclaw** colours: kora,
balafon, ney and darbuka, talking drum, udu, hot wind and cicadas. The brass
belongs to the Gathering itself — to both halves at once.
