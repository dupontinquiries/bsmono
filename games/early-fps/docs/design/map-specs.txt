To build competitive maps, traditional terrain smoothing algorithms (like Gaussian blurs on heightmaps) are actually the enemy. Generic smoothing creates rolling, organic hills that look pretty but play terribly—they break player momentum, cause erratic camera bobbing, and make it impossible to place clean geometry.

In a tactical shooter, terrain smoothing must be Intent-Driven. The FSM shouldn't just average nearby vertices; it should evaluate why the terrain is uneven and smooth it according to strict gameplay rules.

Here are four rule-based Terrain Smoothing FSMs designed to create hyper-playable, physically grounded battlefields.

1. The "ADS-Compliant" Flattener (Micro-Terrain FSM)
Input: Raw, noisy heightmap vertices generated by standard Perlin or fractal noise.
Purpose: To eliminate micro-bumps that would disrupt a player's Aim Down Sights (ADS) or cause them to get stuck on tiny inclines, while preserving deliberate elevation changes.

State 0 (Slope Vector Analysis): The FSM scans the terrain in a grid pattern. At each vertex, it calculates the angle between the current vertex and its immediate neighbors in the four cardinal directions.
State 1 (The 15-Degree Threshold): If the angle is below 15 degrees, the FSM locks the vertex—it remains "raw" and untouched. If the angle exceeds 15 degrees but spans a short distance (under 2 meters), it is flagged as "combat noise."
State 2 (Targeted Averaging): Instead of a blanket blur, the FSM only targets the "combat noise" vertices. It averages their height down to match the lowest neighboring vertex. It does not average upward. (It is always better for gameplay to carve a small flat shelf into a hill than to build a tiny, awkward bump).
State 3 (Material Masking): The FSM applies a material mask: the newly flattened vertices receive a "Compacted Earth" or "Paved" texture, while the untouched raw vertices retain "Grass" or "Scree." This visually communicates to the player: "This is a clean surface for gunfights."
Output: Terrain that looks natural from a distance but provides hundreds of invisible, perfectly flat micro-platforms for fluid combat.
2. The "Prone Ledge" Hardener (Inverse-Smoothing FSM)
Input: A high-elevation ridge line or cliff face.
Purpose: To create the perfect sniper or LMG prone position. Standard smoothing would round off the edge of a cliff, making it look like a smooth hill and removing the cover. This FSM sharpens the downward edge while smoothing the upward approach.

State 0 (Crest Detection): The FSM isolates a continuous line of vertices that represent the highest local elevation before a severe drop-off.
State 1 (Lip Extrusion): The FSM identifies the vertices on the "enemy-facing" side of the crest. It aggressively lowers these vertices to create an abrupt, sheer drop (a 70+ degree angle). This creates a hard visual horizon line and an impenetrable physical wall for incoming fire.
State 2 (Rear-Approach Smoothing): The FSM identifies the vertices on the "friendly-facing" side of the crest (the side players will walk up). It applies a heavy smoothing algorithm here, creating a gentle, 5-to-10-degree ramp.
State 3 (The "Invisible Bench"): Exactly one meter behind the sharp lip, the FSM forces a perfectly flat, 1.5-meter wide shelf.
Output: A ridge where a player can sprint up a smooth back-ramp, seamlessly drop into the invisible 1.5m bench, and go prone perfectly flush against a sharp cliff lip, with their crosshairs completely stable.
3. The "Walled Garden" Carver (Routing FSM)
Input: Terrain situated between two POIs (e.g., a Spawn Point FSM and a Bunker FSM).
Purpose: To carve logical, intentional pathways through chaotic terrain, ensuring players naturally flow toward the objectives without needing minimap arrows.

State 0 (Vector Calculation): The FSM draws a straight mathematical line between the center-points of the two POIs.
State 1 (Obstruction Query): It analyzes all terrain vertices along this line. If a vertex is higher than the player's eye-level, the path is considered "blocked."
State 2 (Dynamic Trenching): If blocked, the FSM does not raise the player or lower the obstruction. Instead, it carves a trench. It lowers the vertices along the vector line by 1.5 meters.
State 3 (Berm Smoothing): To make the trench look like a natural earthwork or dried riverbed rather than a laser-cut canyon, the FSM applies a cubic bezier curve to the transition edges (the berms). The edges are smoothed perfectly, while the bottom of the trench is left strictly flat.
State 4 (Drainage Logic): The FSM tilts the flat bottom of the trench by a fraction of a degree. It then spawns a "Puddle" decal at the lowest vertex.
Output: A natural-looking depression in the earth that subtly guides player movement and provides a covered approach lane.
4. The "Geometry Socket" Localizer (POI Integration FSM)
Input: The fully smoothed terrain + any placed POI primitives (like the "Sentinel Node" bunker from the previous prompt).
Purpose: To eliminate the "floating building" or "clipping geometry" artifact that plagues procedural generation, ensuring objects look physically grounded.

State 0 (Bounding Box Projection): When a POI primitive is placed, the FSM projects its bottom bounding box directly downward onto the terrain heightmap.
State 1 (Absolute Snapping): Any terrain vertex that falls inside the projected bounding box has its height immediately overridden and locked to the exact bottom face of the POI primitive.
State 2 (The Transition Grade): The FSM looks at the vertices immediately outside the bounding box. It compares their height to the newly flattened "socket" area. It runs a localized smoothing pass strictly on these outer vertices to create a smooth, 45-degree ramp transitioning from the raw earth up to the base of the building.
State 3 (Foundation Splay): The FSM spawns a slightly larger, transparent "shadow" mesh (like a concrete foundation or packed dirt pad) that extends 0.5 meters beyond the POI's footprint, overlapping the transition grade to hide the seam between geometry and terrain.
Output: A bunker or tower that looks like it was physically built into the earth, with the dirt realistically grading up to its walls, completely free of z-fighting or awkward floating.
