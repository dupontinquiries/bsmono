# The raids

Nineteen bosses, three phases each, generated by `tools/gen_raids.py` (or
`assetkit-cli raids`) from the fiction in `game-model-tools/plans/raids-and-relics.md`.

## How a raid is built

Each boss's **mechanic is derived from its lore**, not assigned at random. The
fiction already implies how the fight should work, and every raid records the
premise line its mechanic came from so the reasoning stays inspectable — if you
disagree with a mapping, you can see exactly what drove it.

The four mechanics are the engine's existing `raid_enc_t.mech` values:

| code | name | what it asks of the player |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | PURGE | clear every hostile in the zone |
| 1 | GROUND | anti-air; flyers are forced down and fight on foot |
| 2 | AIR | elevated; reachable only by flight or grapple |
| 3 | SYNC | plates need several units standing together |

Three rules the generator enforces:

* **The boss's own mechanic appears on at least one encounter per phase.** A phase
  where everything is PURGE plays identically to a phase with no mechanic at all.
* **Every map a phase references must exist.** This check was added because the
  hand-written raid plan pointed at `kuiper` and `unending`, which had never been
  defined — two of three phases were loading an undefined theme.
* **SYNC bosses finish at `unending`.** The plate mechanic wants the arena where
  the horizon curves the wrong way.

Phases run **The Approach → The Gauntlet → The <finale>**, four encounters each,
the first of which is `kind 1` (the anchor) and the rest `kind 0`.

Regenerate with a different layout at any time:

    assetkit-cli raids --raid gigadrill --phases 3 --seed 7

## Playthrough — what a raid actually asks of you

A raid is three gated phases. You cannot skip ahead: `g_raid_phase` only advances
when the current phase's encounters are cleared, and `g_raid_over` is set when the
last one falls.

### The Approach

Four encounters on a map chosen away from the boss's own arena, so the raid opens
somewhere that does **not** yet play by the boss's rules. The anchor encounter is
`kind 1`; the rest are ordinary. Expect one or two encounters carrying the boss's
mechanic as a warning of what the finale will demand.

This phase is where you learn the mechanic cheaply. A GROUND encounter here costs
you a flyer for thirty seconds; the same encounter in the finale costs the run.

### The Gauntlet

Four more, on a second map. The mechanic density rises — this is the phase most
likely to have three of four encounters running the boss's own rule — and there is
no terrain advantage carried over, because the map changed.

The design intent: by the end of the Gauntlet you should have solved the mechanic
*in general* rather than in one arena.

### The finale

Named for the boss's title — The Reactor, The Core Piercer, The Ocean King — and
sited either at `unending` (every SYNC boss) or on the most extreme map the seed
picked. All four encounters, with the boss's mechanic guaranteed.

### Reading a mechanic

**PURGE** is the null case: clear the zone. It is the baseline the other three are
measured against, and a phase of nothing but PURGE is a phase with no idea in it.

**GROUND** forces flyers down. If your loadout leans on flight this is the
mechanic that punishes it, and the counter is to bring something that fights well
on foot rather than to out-fly it.

**AIR** puts the encounter somewhere you cannot walk. It is the inverse test, and
the two together mean a raid roster wants both capabilities rather than one
specialisation.

**SYNC** requires `min_units` — three, where PURGE encounters need one — standing
on plates together. It is the only mechanic that cannot be solved by a single
strong unit, which is why the resonance and flood bosses use it: Speakerman
fractures planets by matching tectonic resonance, and resonance is a thing several
bodies do together or not at all.

### Why the mechanic fits the fiction

Worth spelling out for three, because the mapping is the interesting part:

* **Cherno Alpha** — "if it ever stops walking, the reactor reaches criticality."
  GROUND, because the fight is about a thing that must keep moving, and the arena
  punishes players who try to body-block it.
* **Gigadrill** — "it drilled through moons, worlds, dwarf planets." AIR, because
  it leaves the arena floor; the phases alternate surface and undercut.
* **Trenchmaster** — "the ocean king; the water rises to meet it." SYNC, because
  high ground shrinks over the fight and the plates that remain need holding
  together.
