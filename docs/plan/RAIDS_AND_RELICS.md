For example, defeating the Scorpion Commander might not just unlock a drone factory ability. It could unlock the ability to manufacture autonomous construction units for your expedition. Those units can establish forward outposts in newly discovered systems, repair derelict space bridges, harvest asteroid resources while you're away, or even slowly reconstruct abandoned Cybertronian facilities over days.

Similarly, recovering the Speakerman's technology might let your expedition deploy long-range resonance beacons. At first they simply reveal hidden caches. Later, a network of them can triangulate faint signals from relics buried inside rogue planets or drifting inside nebulae, opening entirely new patrol regions.

That means progression has three interconnected scales:

Personal engineering – your Transformer's body, relics, alternate forms, and combat abilities.
Expedition engineering – your flagship, drone fleet, fabrication bays, research labs, and forward outposts.
Civilizational engineering – restoring ancient infrastructure like orbital rings, planetary foundries, relay arrays, and eventually the systems protecting the Ninth Forge.

Those layers reinforce each other. Stronger personal relics let you tackle harder expeditions. Better expedition infrastructure uncovers more ancient technology. Restoring ancient infrastructure reveals new sectors, new raids, and more of the mystery behind the relics. The world then feels like it's being progressively reclaimed rather than simply consumed for loot.
